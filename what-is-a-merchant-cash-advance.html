What is a merchant cash advance, and how is it repaid?

Plain answers on how an MCA works, what it costs, and when it is the right tool for a business, and when it is not.

By TMF Team Last updated 25 October 2026 Reading time 4 minutes

Short answer

A merchant cash advance (MCA) is not a loan. A funder buys part of your future revenue at a discount and pays you the cash now. You repay it as an agreed percentage of your card and bank receipts, so remittances rise and fall with sales. Through TMF Team, advances run from $5,000 to $2 million.

How a merchant cash advance works

The funder looks at your recent business bank statements, agrees an amount to advance, and agrees the total it will collect back, called the purchased amount. It then collects a fixed percentage of your revenue until that amount has been remitted.

Because the funder is buying receivables rather than lending money, there is no interest rate and no fixed monthly instalment. That is the main difference from a term loan.

Other names for a merchant cash advance

The same product is sold under several names, which is why comparing offers can be confusing. You will see it called a business cash advance, an MCA loan (even though it is not a loan), revenue-based financing, a sales-based advance or, at TMF Team, a cash injection.

Whatever the name, check three things in the offer: how much you receive, the total amount the funder will collect, and what share of your revenue it collects and how often.

How an MCA is priced

An MCA is priced with a factor rate applied to the advance, not an interest rate. The funder sets it after underwriting your file. Your offer shows the remittance structure and the total cost of capital in writing before you accept anything.

Pricing is driven mainly by your revenue and the health of your bank statements. Personal credit affects the price more than it affects whether you qualify.

What happens if sales slow down

Remittances are a percentage of revenue, so slower days mean smaller remittances. Payoff simply takes longer. That flexibility is the main reason businesses with uneven sales choose an MCA over a fixed-payment loan.

How fast an MCA funds

You apply on day one. Offers typically come back within 2 to 4 hours of a complete file, and funding usually lands within 4 to 24 hours of applying, often a couple of hours after you accept an offer. See how fast a business can get funded for the full timeline.

When an MCA fits, and when it does not

SituationIs an MCA a good fit?
You need capital this weekOften yes. It is one of the fastest routes.
Sales are steady but personal credit is imperfectOften yes. It is underwritten mainly on revenue.
You are buying property or long-life equipmentUsually no. Look at an SBA 504 loan.
What you already owe on advances outweighs what you bring inNo. Talk about consolidation or a payoff structure instead.
You need cash on and off, not onceConsider a business line of credit.

What you need to apply

Basic business information and four months of business bank statements, which are required in every state. The application is four steps and takes about four minutes. Start the application.
