How it works

One limit, used again and again.

A line of credit is not a lump sum. It sits there until you need it, which makes it a good fit for uneven months, seasonal stock and bills that arrive before the customer pays.

  1. Step 1

    Apply once

    Basic business details and four months of business bank statements. About four minutes.

  2. Step 2

    Get your limit

    Funders review the file and offer a limit. You see the cost and the terms before you accept.

  3. Step 3

    Draw when needed

    Take part of the limit or all of it. Cost is charged on the amount drawn, not on the whole line.

  4. Step 4

    Repay and reuse

    Each repayment frees that room again, so the line keeps working for the business.

Who it suits

For cash flow that comes and goes.

A line works best when the need repeats: you know money will be short for a few weeks at a time, again and again.

  • Busy and slow seasons

    Seasonal businesses

  • Pay suppliers first

    Inventory & stock

  • Customers pay in 30–90 days

    Waiting on invoices

  • A cushion, not a loan

    Unplanned costs

Know what it costs

Line or lump sum?

If you need one amount once, a cash injection or a loan is usually simpler. If the need keeps coming back, a line saves you from applying again each time.

Limit
$10K to $250K, set by the funder from your revenue, time in business and credit.
Cost
Charged on what you draw. Your offer shows the rate or fee and any draw costs before you accept.
Repayment
Usually weekly or monthly on each draw. Repaid amounts become available to draw again.
Decision
Varies by funder. Some answer the same day, others take a few days.

Terms depend on underwriting, your bank statements and the funder. Not available in every state.
