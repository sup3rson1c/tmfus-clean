Business funding options: which one fits your business?

Small business funding comes in a handful of shapes. Each one is built for a different job, and picking the wrong shape costs more than picking the wrong lender.

By TMF Team Last updated 25 October 2026 Reading time 6 minutes

Short answer

The main small business funding options are SBA loans (cheapest, 30 to 90 days), business lines of credit (draw as you need, pay on what you use), merchant cash advances (fastest, repaid from sales), and home equity lines (cheaper, secured on your home). The right one depends on how fast you need the money and what it is for.

Business loans vs loan alternatives

A business loan is borrowed money repaid on a schedule with interest. SBA 7(a) and 504 loans are loans. A merchant cash advance is not a loan: a funder buys part of your future revenue and collects a share of your sales. A line of credit is a revolving limit you draw from. A home equity line is a loan against your house that you can use for the business.

Searching for "small business loans" will show you all of these, so it helps to know which shape you are looking at before comparing prices.

How to choose in three questions

  • How soon do you need it? This week rules out SBA. Today or tomorrow points to a merchant cash advance.
  • What is it for? Property and long-life equipment point to SBA 504. Payroll, inventory and short gaps point to a line of credit or an advance.
  • What can you put up? Home equity usually prices better than unsecured business credit, but it puts the house at risk.

What about grants?

Grants are money you do not repay, usually from government programs or foundations, and they are competitive, slow and tied to specific purposes. TMF Team does not arrange grants. If you need capital on a deadline, the options above are the realistic ones.
