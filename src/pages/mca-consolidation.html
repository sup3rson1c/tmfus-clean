What can you do about stacked merchant cash advances?

When several advances are taking a share of the same sales, adding another one usually makes it worse. These are the realistic ways out.

By TMF Team Last updated 25 October 2026 Reading time 4 minutes

Short answer

If what you owe on existing advances outweighs what you bring in, a new advance is the wrong tool. The realistic options are a consolidation, using home equity to clear positions, negotiating a restructure with the funders, or an SBA 7(a) loan for eligible purposes to free up cash flow. SBA 7(a) cannot refinance the advances themselves.

Signs you are over-leveraged

  • Several funders are collecting from the same account every week.
  • Remittances are taking most of what comes in.
  • The funding calculator tells you that you are over-leveraged: what you owe is larger than your revenue supports advancing.

Why SBA cannot pay off advances

Under the SBA's lending rules, SOP 50 10 8, effective June 1, 2025, merchant cash advances and factoring agreements are not eligible for refinancing with SBA 7(a) proceeds, across every 7(a) variant. There is no workaround inside the program.
