How do you get an SBA loan?

SBA loans are the cheapest money most small businesses can get. They are also the most paperwork. Here is what qualifying actually involves.

By TMF Team Last updated 25 October 2026 Reading time 5 minutes

Short answer

To get an SBA loan you apply through an SBA lender, not the SBA itself. You need a for-profit US business that is small by SBA size standards, can show it can repay, and cannot get comparable credit on reasonable terms elsewhere. Expect a personal guarantee from 20% owners, heavy paperwork and 30 to 90 days to funding.

Who qualifies

  • A for-profit business based and trading in the US or its territories.
  • Small by the SBA's size standards, which vary by industry and are set by revenue or employee count.
  • Not an ineligible business type, such as lending, gambling or speculation.
  • Passes the "credit elsewhere" test: comparable credit on reasonable terms is not available from non-government sources.
  • Creditworthy and able to repay. Lenders add their own credit standards on top of the SBA's floor.

Documents you will need

  • Business and personal tax returns, usually three years.
  • Year-to-date profit and loss statement and balance sheet.
  • Business bank statements.
  • A personal financial statement for each owner of 20% or more.
  • A debt schedule listing every existing obligation.
  • Use of proceeds: exactly where the money goes.
  • Business licences, entity documents and leases.

The 504 down payment

A 504 is three pieces: a bank lends 50%, a Certified Development Company funds 40% through an SBA-backed debenture, and you put in 10%. That rises to 15% if the business is under two years old or the property is special-purpose, and 20% if both apply.

How long it takes

Commonly 30 to 90 days from a complete application to funding. If you need capital this week, an SBA loan is the wrong instrument; a merchant cash advance or a line of credit fits better.
