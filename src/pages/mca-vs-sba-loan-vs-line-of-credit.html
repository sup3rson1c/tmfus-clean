MCA vs SBA loan vs line of credit: which fits your business?

Four common ways to fund a small business, compared on what actually decides the choice: how fast you need the money, what it is for, and what you can put up.

By TMF Team Last updated 25 October 2026 Reading time 5 minutes

Short answer

Pick by speed and purpose. A merchant cash advance funds in hours to a day and suits urgent working capital. An SBA loan is the cheapest but takes 30 to 90 days, so it suits planned investments. A line of credit suits recurring cash needs. A HELOC is often cheaper still, but it is secured on your home.

If you need the money this week

An SBA loan is the wrong instrument: the paperwork and the government guarantee take 30 to 90 days. A merchant cash advance is built for speed, with offers typically within 2 to 4 hours of a complete file.

If you are buying property or equipment

An SBA 504 loan is built for buildings, land and long-life equipment, with long fixed rates. A 7(a) is the flexible option for working capital, equipment, real estate, eligible refinancing and changes of ownership. Some deals use both.

If you need cash on and off

A business line of credit gives you a limit to draw from. You pay only on what you have drawn: a $100K line with $20K drawn costs you on the $20K. Some funders also charge a fee per draw or a small maintenance fee, and your offer lists every cost.

If you own your home

Home equity usually prices better than unsecured business credit, which is why many owners use a HELOC for business capital. The trade is real: the loan is secured against where you live, so a bad quarter in the business becomes a risk to the house. Price both before you decide.

Can you have an MCA and get an SBA loan?

Possibly. 7(a) proceeds cannot be used to refinance merchant cash advances or factoring agreements under the SBA's current rules, but having open advances does not automatically disqualify you from an SBA loan for another purpose. What matters is whether your cash flow still services the new debt with the advances in place.
