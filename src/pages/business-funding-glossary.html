Business funding terms, defined plainly.

The words in a funding offer, explained in a sentence each, so you know exactly what you are reading before you sign.

By TMF Team Last updated 25 October 2026 Reading time 5 minutes

Short answer

Most funding offers use a handful of terms: a factor rate (how an MCA is priced), the purchased amount (what the funder collects back), remittances (each payment, as a share of revenue), positions (open advances), and for loans, a personal guarantee and collateral. Each is defined below.

Terms A to Z

Business line of credit
A revolving limit a business can draw from, repay and draw again, paying only on what is drawn.
Capital injection
New money put into a business. In SBA 504 lending, the owner's down payment, usually 10%.
Cash injection
New money put into a business. At TMF Team, the name for its merchant cash advance.
Certified Development Company (CDC)
A nonprofit that funds the 40% SBA-backed piece of a 504 loan.
Collateral
An asset pledged to secure a loan, which the lender can claim if the loan is not repaid.
Consolidation
Replacing or restructuring several existing advances or debts into one arrangement.
Credit elsewhere test
The SBA rule that it only guarantees a loan when comparable credit on reasonable terms is not available from non-government sources.
Draw
Taking money from a line of credit. With a home equity line, the draw period is often five to ten years.
EIN
Employer Identification Number, the federal tax ID for a business. Needed for every TMF Team application.
Factor rate
How a merchant cash advance is priced: a multiple applied to the advance, rather than an interest rate.
Funder
The company that provides the money and makes the credit decision. A broker presents your file to funders.
Merchant cash advance (MCA)
A purchase of future receivables. The funder pays now and collects an agreed percentage of revenue until the purchased amount is remitted.
Over-leveraged
When what a business owes on existing positions is larger than its revenue supports advancing.
Personal guarantee
A promise by an owner to repay a business debt personally. SBA loans require one from owners of 20% or more.
Position
An open merchant cash advance. "Stacking" means holding several positions at once.
Purchased amount
The total a merchant cash advance funder collects back from your revenue.
Remittance
Each payment collected toward a merchant cash advance, set as a percentage of revenue so it falls when sales fall.
Revenue-based financing
Funding repaid as a share of revenue. Often used as another name for a merchant cash advance.
SBA 7(a)
The SBA's main loan program, up to $5M, for working capital, equipment, real estate, eligible refinancing and changes of ownership.
SBA 504
SBA loans for land, buildings and long-life equipment, built as 50% bank, 40% CDC and usually 10% owner.
