What is a working capital loan, and which kind fits?

Working capital funding pays for the day-to-day running of the business. Here is how the main options compare.

By TMF Team Last updated 25 October 2026 Reading time 4 minutes

Short answer

Working capital is the cash a business uses to run day to day: payroll, inventory, rent and suppliers. A working capital loan covers gaps in that cash. The main options are a business line of credit for recurring needs, a merchant cash advance for fast one-off needs, and an SBA 7(a) loan when you can wait 30 to 90 days for a lower cost.

What working capital is

In accounting terms, working capital is what the business owns in the short term, such as cash, money owed by customers and stock, minus what it owes in the short term. In practice it is the money that keeps the doors open between paying your costs and getting paid.

A profitable business can still run short. If you pay suppliers in 30 days and your customers pay you in 60, you are funding that gap yourself until the money arrives.

What SBA 504 cannot do

An SBA 504 loan is built for buildings, land and long-life equipment. It cannot fund working capital or inventory. For those, look at 7(a), a line of credit or an advance.
