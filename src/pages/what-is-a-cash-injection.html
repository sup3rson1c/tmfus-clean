What is a cash injection for a business?

The phrase means different things depending on who says it. Here are the three meanings you are most likely to meet.

By TMF Team Last updated 25 October 2026 Reading time 3 minutes

Short answer

A cash injection is new money put into a business to cover a gap or fund growth. It can come from the owners, from investors, or from outside funding. In SBA lending, your down payment is called your capital injection. At TMF Team, "cash injection" is the name for its merchant cash advance: $5,000 to $2 million, repaid from sales.

Three meanings of cash injection

Where you see itWhat it means
General business useAny new money put into the business: owners' own money, investors buying a stake, or borrowed funds
SBA 504 loansThe owner's down payment, usually 10% of the project, 15% for a business under two years old or special-purpose property, 20% if both apply
TMF TeamA merchant cash advance: a funder buys part of your future revenue and collects a share of your sales

When a business needs one

  • Covering a cash-flow gap while customers pay late.
  • Buying stock or materials for a large order.
  • Getting through a slow season.
  • Taking an opportunity that will not wait for a bank.
