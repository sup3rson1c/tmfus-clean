How does a business line of credit work?

A line of credit is the most flexible way to fund a business. It is also the most misunderstood. Here is how the limit, the draws and the cost actually work.

By TMF Team Last updated 25 October 2026 Reading time 6 minutes

Short answer

A business line of credit gives you a credit limit you can draw from whenever the business needs cash. You pay only on what you have drawn, and as you repay, that amount becomes available to draw again. Through TMF Team, lines run from $10,000 to $250,000. It suits recurring or uneven cash needs better than a one-off lump sum.

The draw and repay cycle

Think of the limit as a reservoir. You draw what you need, repay it, and the space refills. Here is a simple example with no prices, because those come from the funder:

StepDrawnStill available
Line opens$0$100,000
Draw $20,000 for inventory$20,000$80,000
Draw $15,000 for payroll$35,000$65,000
Repay $20,000 as sales come in$15,000$85,000

You are charged on the drawn balance, not the limit. In the example, the cost runs on $20,000, then $35,000, then $15,000, never on the full $100,000.

What a line of credit costs

Cost is charged on what you have drawn. Some funders also charge a fee per draw or a small maintenance fee. Your offer lists every cost in writing before you sign, so compare the whole picture, not just the headline rate.

How repayment works

The funder sets the repayment schedule, and it is in the offer: how often payments are taken, how much, and how each draw is repaid. The same offer says how a draw is requested and how long the money takes to reach your business account.

What you need to qualify

Basic business information and four months of business bank statements. The application is four steps and takes about four minutes. Checking what you qualify for does not affect your credit; a funder may run a credit check before making a final offer.

Good uses for a line

  • Covering payroll while you wait for customers to pay.
  • Buying inventory ahead of a busy season.
  • Smoothing the gap between a big job's costs and its payment.
  • Keeping a cushion you only pay for when you use it.

A line secured on your home

A home equity line of credit works the same way, draw and repay, but it is secured against your home, runs up to $750,000 and is priced off your credit score. It is usually cheaper, and the risk is the house.
