Can you use a HELOC to fund your business?

Yes, and many owners do. Here is how it works, why it is often cheaper, and the risk you should weigh first.

By TMF Team Last updated 25 October 2026 Reading time 4 minutes

Short answer

Yes. A home equity line of credit lets you borrow against the equity in your home and use it for business capital. It usually prices better than unsecured business credit. The trade-off is that the line is secured on where you live, so a bad quarter in the business becomes a risk to the house. Through TMF Team, lines run up to $750,000.

How a HELOC works

You borrow against the equity in your home. During the draw period, often five to ten years, you take what you need up to your limit and typically pay interest only on what you have used. When the repayment period starts, the line closes to new draws and you repay principal plus interest on a schedule.

How it is priced

A home equity line is priced off your credit score, and the rate improves at roughly 660, 700, 740 and 780. That is the opposite of a merchant cash advance, which is priced mainly off revenue.

The risk to weigh

The loan is secured against your home. If the business struggles, the debt does not go away with it. Weigh a HELOC against an unsecured product before you decide, and ask for both to be priced so you can compare.

Using home equity to clear advances

If you own property, a HELOC is often materially cheaper than a merchant cash advance and can be used to clear open positions. SBA 7(a) proceeds cannot be used for that under current SBA rules, which makes home equity one of the lower-cost ways to clear stacked advances.
