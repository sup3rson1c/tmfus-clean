The programs

Two programs, two jobs.

7(a) is the flexible, general-purpose program. 504 is built for buildings and long-life equipment. Most businesses fit one far better than the other.

7(a)

Up to $5M

The SBA's primary and most widely used program.

Use it for

  • Working capital
  • Real estate acquisition, refinancing and improvements
  • Machinery, equipment, furniture, fixtures and supplies
  • Refinancing eligible existing business debt
  • Changes of ownership

504

Up to $5.5M SBA portion

Long-term, fixed-rate financing for major fixed assets.

Use it for

  • Buying land or buildings, construction and renovation
  • Long-term machinery and equipment with a useful life of 10+ years
  • Refinancing qualifying debt tied to those assets

Not for

  • Working capital or inventory
  • Repaying non-qualified debt, or speculation

How 504 is built

The 50 / 40 / 10 split.

A 504 project is financed in three parts, which is why the down payment is lower than a conventional commercial mortgage.

50%

Senior lender, such as a bank or credit union, in first lien position

40%

CDC and SBA, through a debenture, in second position

10%

You, as the capital injection

  • 15% down if the business is under two years old, or the property is special-purpose.
  • 20% down if both apply.
  • Terms of 10, 20 or 25 years, matched to the useful life of the asset.

Important

7(a) can't pay off cash advances.

Effective June 1, 2025, merchant cash advances and factoring agreements are not eligible for refinancing with SBA 7(a) proceeds.

So what can you actually do?

  • Consolidate outside the SBA

    A conventional consolidation can combine open positions into one structure.

  • Use home equity

    If you own property, a home equity line can pay balances down at a lower cost.

  • Negotiate with funders

    Payoff or restructuring terms can sometimes be agreed directly.

  • Use 7(a) where eligible

    Funding eligible needs through 7(a) can free up cash flow for existing positions.
